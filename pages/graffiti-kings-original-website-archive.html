ORIGINAL GRAFFITI KINGS WEBSITE ARCHIVE

Historical guide to the former Graffiti Kings .co.uk website: 2019–2020 Wayback snapshots, mural services, founder SER, campaigns and the transition to WAX projects.

Historical record — not a booking website. This page documents the earlier Graffiti Kings commercial street-art website, principally through its 2019–2020 Wayback captures and other dated sources. The former commercial operation is described as closed on the later legacy domain. Historic services, clients and announcements are not current offers, endorsements or proof of a continuing contract.

The original Graffiti Kings website

Before the WAX, GKniftyHEADS and Crypto Moonboys chapters, graffitikings.co.uk presented Graffiti Kings as a London-based graffiti and street-art company associated with founder Darren Cullen, the writer known as SER. Its archived homepage promoted art production, public commissions, live events and workshops, with links into a project portfolio. The snapshot is a view of a commercial website at one moment in time, not a definitive list of everything the group produced.

The old site is significant for more than nostalgia. It is an original publisher record that connects the Graffiti Kings name to real painted work and historic business activity. The modern wiki history describes a later fictional and digital world; this section keeps the physical record and subsequent world-building distinct.

Services recorded on the former business website

Historic Graffiti Kings publicity described a practical production company, not only a street-art collective. A historical meta-description names murals, live art, graffiti workshops, team building and bedroom walls; the 2013 directory copy adds further categories. These are records of what was offered or advertised then, not services available for booking today.

Murals & painted environments

Walls, large-format artwork, bedrooms, offices, restaurants and retail environments. The original website treated painted surfaces as commissions with distinct locations and audiences.

Advertising & brand campaigns

Campaign murals, public activations and temporary promotional artwork. Named productions appear in the historic project directory.

Live art, workshops & education

Participatory graffiti workshops, team-building activities and live demonstrations formed part of the old published service mix; programme dates and continuing availability require separate evidence.

Film, music & set design

Decorative backdrops, street-style sets and music-video painting appear in promotional descriptions. A dated Stefflon Don project page provides a specific historical example.

Graffiti Kings also published radio and music activity. For that adjacent history use the radio and audio evidence directory, rather than implying an archived stream still operates.

Original portfolio and campaigns

The old work index includes entries associated with entertainment, television, drinks, fashion and commercial advertising. Four dated project-page snapshots were identified in earlier archive research: Stefflon Don, Jack Daniel's, Marvel Studios and Game of Thrones. The earlier index also mentions The Shape of Water, The Maze Runner and TGI Fridays. Each needs to be read in the context of the archived page's own claims.

The standalone legacy campaign register separates directly dated archival links from names seen only in an index and from later, self-attributed production credits. It includes original Wayback links alongside individual summaries and provenance labels, so source strength is visible rather than assumed.

A later Graffiti Artists Worldwide production portfolio separately uses the phrase “Produced for Graffiti Kings” for several works. That is a linked collaborator claim, not independent confirmation of every brand's client relationship or of continued work today.

Artists, attribution and the street roots

Darren Cullen / SER: identified by interviews and period Graffiti Kings publicity as the founder or operator of the commercial brand. His earlier graffiti writing, community work and the 2012 Olympic-period records are covered in the documented SER timeline and wiki biography.

Charlie Buster: an artist connected with the wider Graffiti Kings studio history through his own NO BALL GAMES artist account. His specific dates, independent works and Grime Art line are set out separately in the Charlie Buster art record. An archive entry for the company does not establish that every artist worked on every campaign.

Other historic collaborators are not assigned credit for individual murals without a corresponding named source. Images are not copied from Wayback: missing archive images are intentionally left with the archive owner.

Where the legacy connects to the present

The transition is chronological, not a claim that the 2019 business website was already a blockchain business. The older site documents physical art production. Graffiti Kings' later WAX collections, WAXP reference and GKniftyHEADS / GraffPUNKS / Crypto Moonboys connections cover subsequent digital projects and their separate supporting records.

Explore the connected evidence chain: 2019 commercial portfolio → SER's documented career → external news and source network → later NFT collection records. Fictional Year 3008 events belong to the wiki canon, not to this historical work register.

Sources, omissions and archival limits

  • Original snapshot: a timestamped Wayback link is the historical site's own publication, not a neutral third-party audit. Some links, pictures and styles may be missing or replay an adjacent date.
  • Period publication: an ITV report or historical press item may independently establish a particular commission; it does not verify every logo on a past portfolio page.
  • Self-published or related portfolio: useful for identifying projects but distinguish “produced for” or “worked with” statements from contracts, invoices and client approval.
  • Completeness: these seven directly dated entry points do not claim to cover every archived URL. The Internet Archive's own guidance explains that captures can be incomplete.

The full evidence notes, capture URLs and open verification items live in the repository's legacy archive source register once merged. If an individual Wayback snapshot fails to replay, use the archive URL history browser to locate an alternative capture; do not silently substitute the current commercial website.
