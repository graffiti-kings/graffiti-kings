Purpose of This Page

This page defines how new lore can be added to the Graffiti Kings, GKniftyHEADS, GraffPUNKS and Crypto Moonboys wiki. The archive is designed to grow, but it is not an open dumping ground for random fantasy writing. Every new addition must connect back to the W81 source material, the 40-faction structure, the Block Topia / City Block Topia setting, the street-rooted Graffiti Kings identity, or the holder-built expansion model described across the canon files.

The wiki exists because the universe is larger than a normal brand page. It contains street history, blockchain history, faction systems, game ideas, NFT roles, user-built IP, future-history, digital agents, archive intelligences, and characters who operate across the street, chain and Grid. New contributions are welcome only when they strengthen that structure instead of replacing it.

The working standard is simple: new lore can extend the world, but it cannot overwrite the root. Graffiti Kings remains the street foundation. GKniftyHEADS remains the Web3 memory and identity layer. GraffPUNKS remain digital renegades, radio signals and rebel-coded operators. Crypto Moonboys remains the wider universe container. The W81 archive remains the canon base that all new pages must obey.

Canon First Rule

Every accepted piece of lore must pass the canon-first rule. That means it must either be directly supported by W81 material or clearly marked as a community expansion that does not contradict W81. A page cannot claim a new founder, rewrite the role of Darren Cullen, replace the Graffiti Kings origin, invent a new master faction above the 40 factions, or remove the importance of Block Topia, the Grid, SAM, GK Brain, HODL WARS, Hard Fork logic, street memory or chain memory.

Canon-first does not mean every new paragraph has to be copied from the archive. It means the new idea must sit inside the archive’s logic. A new character can be built as a faction runner, grid courier, prophet, graffiti operator, HODL warrior, moon-signal watcher, burn-site mechanic, NFT relic holder, radio pirate, AI archive ghost or Block Topia citizen. That works because those categories already fit the universe. A random dragon kingdom, medieval empire, superhero multiverse, generic alien race or unrelated token tribe does not fit unless the W81 archive already gives a route for it.

The strongest submissions are not short. They explain who the figure is, where they operate, what faction they connect to, what their conflict is, what they protect, what they want, what visual symbols define them, what chain or street mechanic they touch, and how they create future story hooks for games, comics, NFT bios, wiki entries, events or holder missions.

What Fits the Archive

Accepted lore should feel like it belongs to a graffiti-born blockchain universe. It should carry the pressure of the street and the strange logic of the chain. The tone can be serious, raw, funny, mythic, technical or chaotic, but it should not become generic fantasy detached from the brand. The most useful submissions build from one or more of the following canon layers:

LayerAccepted Direction
Street LayerGraffiti missions, crews, wall memory, tags, legal walls, underground culture, London roots, grime pressure, street symbols and real-world creative proof.
Chain LayerNFT ownership, burn mechanics, token rituals, wallet identities, WAX collection memory, free claims, holder roles and blockchain archive traces.
Faction LayerCharacters, conflicts, alliances, rituals and missions linked to the 40-faction structure.
Grid LayerBlock Topia, City Block Topia, The Grid, archive signals, corrupted districts, digital ghosts and system-level operators.
Game LayerHODL WARS, Hard Fork Program, faction battles, runner missions, map control, burn/earn/own loops and Telegram-style game ideas.
Archive LayerSAM, GK Brain, Codex relics, scrolls, memory fragments, wiki entries, future-history and canon preservation.

A strong submission should be usable across the wiki. A good faction member can become a character page, a playable game role, an NFT biography, a comic scene, a social post, a Telegram mission, a merch drop, a wall-piece brief or a future holder quest. The archive is not built for disposable paragraphs. It is built for material that can be reused and expanded.

What Gets Rejected

Submissions should be rejected or rewritten when they break the archive. The most common problem is lore that sounds large but has no connection to the actual universe. Big names, gods, empires, planets and wars do not automatically make useful canon. In this wiki, scale has to come from the Graffiti Kings root, the faction map, the blockchain memory layer, the Grid, the year 3008 framing, or the holder-built IP system.

The following material should not be added as canon:

  • New lore that contradicts W81, existing faction pages or the current wiki structure.
  • Random fantasy that ignores Graffiti Kings, GKniftyHEADS, GraffPUNKS, Crypto Moonboys, Block Topia or the 40 factions.
  • Claims that a community character is the true founder, supreme ruler or replacement for the established root figures.
  • Token claims, financial promises, guaranteed rewards, investment language or invented mechanics not supported by the archive.
  • Real-world accusations, private information, defamatory claims or personal attacks.
  • AI filler that reads like a generic fantasy wiki and does not carry street, chain or faction identity.
  • Very short stubs that do not provide enough material to build a serious wiki page.

Rejected does not always mean deleted forever. Some ideas can be rewritten into the correct shape. A generic “space knight” may become a HODLWARRIORS satellite guard. A random hacker can become a GraffPUNKS pirate-signal operator. A magic object can become a Codex relic, burn key, wallet shard, block-seed or Grid artefact. The question is always whether the idea can be pulled back into W81 logic.

Submission Types

The wiki can accept several types of lore. Each type needs a different level of detail. A full character page needs more than a name. A faction expansion needs more than a badge. A game mechanic needs more than a cool phrase. The point is to create usable archive material.

Submission TypeRequired Detail
CharacterName, faction, role, origin, visual markers, personality, conflict, skills, location, alliances, enemies and future story hooks.
Faction ExpansionFaction name, W81 link, belief system, territory, symbols, members, conflict style and relationship to other factions.
NFT BiographyAvatar name, faction, traits, template or collection context, personality, story role, powers, weaknesses and holder-facing utility.
Game MechanicWhat players do, what is earned, what is burned, what is protected, what faction benefits, and how it stays non-financial unless canon says otherwise.
Archive RelicObject name, origin, owner, function, visual form, chain/street link and why the relic matters to the wider memory system.
LocationDistrict name, physical or Grid location, faction control, visual mood, danger level, known events and playable/story function.

Character Rules

Characters are not just names in this universe. They are carriers of faction pressure, memory, style and conflict. A W81-compatible character should have a clear reason to exist. They may be a street-root figure, a faction operator, a grid ghost, a prophet, a runner, a radio voice, a wallet-born avatar, a corrupted AI, a holder legend, a kid myth, a burn-site keeper, a relic thief, a moon-signal watcher or a living bridge between the street and the chain.

The strongest character pages should include long-form sections: identity, faction, visual appearance, origin, role in Block Topia, relationship to the Grid, known missions, personality, powers or skills, weaknesses, important objects, allies, enemies, major quotes, possible game role and how the character can be used by the community. No character page should be left as a one-paragraph profile unless it is deliberately marked as an archive fragment awaiting expansion.

When expanding an NFT avatar into a biography, the character must respect the avatar’s known name, faction, visual traits and collection source. Unknown details can be created, but they must be created inside the W81 world. The biography should feel like it could sit beside GKniftyHEADS, GraffPUNKS, Bitcoin Kid Army, Crypto Moongirls Eternal, NoBallGames Legion, HODLWARRIORS, Nomad Bears or any of the other factions without sounding imported from another franchise.

Faction Rules

The 40 factions are one of the main structures of the W81 archive. They are not only teams. They are belief systems, street tribes, chain cultures, fashion houses, warrior groups, archive watchers, rebel networks, game classes and story engines. A new faction page must explain what the faction believes, where it moves, how it marks territory, how it treats holders, what it fights for and what it refuses to become.

New material should not add a 41st core faction unless the archive is explicitly expanded. Most community ideas should enter as crews, sub-crews, splinter cells, local chapters, rogue operators, districts, missions or relic cults connected to one of the existing 40 factions. This keeps the faction bible intact while allowing the world to grow.

Faction pages should avoid shallow badge lists. Each one needs enough text to support future pages, missions and NFT bios. The standard is full lore: origin, belief, visual code, territory, hierarchy, allies, rivals, known figures, role in HODL WARS, role in the Grid, holder meaning and future expansion hooks.

NFT and Holder Lore

NFT lore in this wiki should treat ownership as memory, not just inventory. A token can be a character, access key, relic, faction mark, burn component, claim marker, badge, street receipt, Grid coordinate, game role or holder identity. W81 repeatedly frames the project as a user-built IP world, so holders should be able to expand characters and stories, but those expansions still need to follow canon rules.

Holder lore can describe how an avatar behaves, what district it patrols, what faction it serves, what relic it carries, what message it spreads, what wall it protects, what game mission it unlocks or what archive fragment it remembers. Holder lore should not promise money, guaranteed token rewards, financial outcomes, special legal rights or permanent commercial rights beyond what the official project explicitly grants.

When an NFT biography is submitted, it should include collection name, avatar name, faction, visible traits, known rarity or template information when available, and a full biography written in the same tone as the rest of the wiki. The biography can create personality and history, but it should not contradict known collection data.

Review Layers

New lore should pass through three checks before being treated as wiki canon. First is the W81 check: does it fit the source archive? Second is the world check: does it fit Graffiti Kings, GKniftyHEADS, GraffPUNKS, Crypto Moonboys, Block Topia, the Grid and the 40 factions? Third is the usefulness check: can this content actually support a page, character, mission, NFT bio, game mechanic, post, comic or archive entry?

Material that passes all three can be written as official wiki content. Material that only partially passes can be marked as draft, rumour, archive fragment, holder myth, rogue signal, corrupted file or pending canon. Material that fails all three should not be added.

Canon is not just what sounds cool. Canon is what strengthens the archive without breaking the root.

Final rule: the wiki is allowed to grow big, but it must grow from the root. The more detailed the submission, the easier it is to turn into a serious page. The weaker the connection to W81, the more likely it needs rewriting before it belongs here.
