Graffiti Kings History and Mythology Timeline

Published street-art history and the long fictional road to Year 3008.

Two timelines, clearly sourced

This page connects documented Graffiti Kings history to a fictional future. The dated artist, exhibition and commercial records below are historical sources. The Covenant, collapsing chains, Block Topia and Year 3008 are mythology, not predictions or completed real events.

Current canon follows GK’s explicit command structure and the reconciled master chronology. Older W81 dates, origins and endings remain attributed witnesses in the Concordance.

The street-art inheritance

Graffiti Kings’ public history supplies the world’s language of walls, reputation, crews, music and authorship. The SER timeline gives the dated record; the original commercial website and archived campaign pages preserve a different part of that history.

Culture becomes a shared world

A public mark can be painted over; a story about that mark can still travel. The fiction carries this practical problem into archives, Chain Scribes, household memories and the Covenant’s duties of witness. Symbolic future roles do not replace real creators’ biographies.

From walls to chain-based art

The real blockchain record includes WAX art accounts, the Graffiti Queens exhibitions, project publishing and collectible plans. Each has its own source trail and date. Buying or holding an artefact does not establish a fictional birth, military rank or unrestricted licence.

The brand becomes a cast

Crypto Moonboys is a creator brand and a fantasy-world cast. Real people can make, inhabit and perform characters, as performers bring a film’s cast to life. The story grows from Graffiti Kings’ street-art roots into many realms, divided records and a struggle over who gets to own a name, a memory or a future.

The Forty and the armies

In Year 3008, crypto rules everyday life. The All City Bulls and Nomad Bears embody the market’s two sides. Both are shadowed 24/7 by undercover Bitcoin Kid Army guards. Alfie “The Bitcoin KiD” Blaze leads that Army and takes orders only from GKniftyHEADS, the underworld good guys above the two market factions and the remaining 37 GK factions.

When the underworld needs a larger army, members of all GK factions unite as the HODL Warriors while keeping their faction identities. The Bitcoin Kid Army and HODL Warriors are armies, not street factions. Every HODL Warrior aspires to become a HODL X Warrior of Block Topia, but that distinct status must be earned.

The current structure is one leading GKniftyHEADS faction, two main market factions and 37 other GK factions. The older website’s numbered roster mixed political factions with royal, military and service traditions. Its numbering is not the present seat list.

The promise of the Hard Fork Games

Queen Sarah P-fly rules Block Topia with her Crypto Moongirls army and the HODL X order. Inside, the engineered Bitcoin X Kids grow within the city’s systems. Some escape and join Alfie’s Army outside. Those movements connect the city and the underworld without making their forces the same.

The Games bring candidates from the Forty into Block Topia. They offer status and survival, while stories of Clone Harvest and neural copying expose a cost the invitation conceals. Jodie, Aleema and their unnamed companion refuse that cost. Their escape is a founding Forkborn account, not a completed escape of every person in the city.

HODL WARS and the unresolved Final Fork

All face NULL THE PROPHET, the Antichain threat that can sever a record from the life it should preserve. Other wars, rivalries and obligations continue. A common enemy does not settle the struggle between the street and the Queen, and the public story leaves the Final Fork unresolved.

The Sacred / Aether-Chain, the World Chain and True Bitcoin Fork have distinct histories. Losing a record, copying a mind and living through a dream are not interchangeable events. Later chronicles that narrate a total victory remain witnesses to their own branch or tradition.

SAM and the household archive

In the House of Rackinsats tradition, Agent SAM develops amid household duties and contested memories. The reconciled household sequence distinguishes the 2036 upload account, 2039 council, 2040 Reset, 2041 bridge, 2042 disappearance and 2045 climax. These belong to that fictional sequence; they are not the universal calendar of every realm.

Other serials place SAM differently. Read the Concordance and House of Rackinsats record before merging a divine portrait, a real person’s name and a household age into one biography.

Where the reader enters

The public story enters a living conflict, not a revealed final ending. Start with the main saga, then follow characters and cultures into accounts with their own viewpoint, dates and limits.

2021: Graffiti Queens and Decentraland

Graffiti Kings' 2021 NFT history includes the Graffiti Queens exhibition in Decentraland, opened on 3 April at Vegas City and curated by Crypto Yuna. Agora Digital's April review documents the open exhibition and more than 280 artworks; Yuna's later podcast reports a different gallery count. A later official Decentraland Art Week announcement separately names Graffiti Kings, James Parkes and Mr C.

These real-world exhibition records are distinct from this wiki's fictional 3008 Graffiti Queens and Crypto Moongirls themes and from the seven named WAX collection accounts.

2022: second Graffiti Queens festival and Alien Worlds grant recognition

Graffiti Queens returned in April 2022 as a second annual virtual-gallery festival, reported by CryptoArtNet with Crypto Yuna as curator and installations mainly in Cryptovoxels. Graffiti Kings' 2021–2022 partner archive also documents SYNC Network announcements and the official Alien Worlds grant-recipient listing naming Darren Cullen and Ian Harrison. These are dated external records, not evidence that a planned 10,000-card NFT programme was fully issued.

Dated external records of the real-world movement

The 2012 policing story is a reported event with contested allegations and is documented separately in the historical record. This sourced date list should not be read as proof of fictional faction events or unverified business claims.

Charlie Buster: Grime Art and the NO BALL GAMES offshoot

Alongside Darren Cullen / SER, the public Graffiti Kings lineage includes Charlie Buster and his independently hosted Saatchi artwork catalogue from 2018–2019. The artist-authored NO BALL GAMES source traces his named Grime Art style to a late-2011 Graffiti Kings studio job; that origin date remains attributed rather than independently verified.

NO BALL GAMES later developed separate WAX $NBG and XRPL XRP KIDs identities. The 2028–2035 Alpha Aaron and FRET arc is fiction, not real blockchain transaction history. The TREEF and Charlie source directories preserve the corresponding links and evidence limits.

WAXP and Graffiti Kings: the infrastructure behind the NFT era

WAX blockchain ownership records became part of the Graffiti Kings project as the movement expanded from physical art to NFTs, GKniftyHEADS digital identities and game-linked assets. WAXP provides the network's native asset, resource access and marketplace settlement; it is not an original Graffiti Kings coin. The WAXP / Graffiti Kings reference documents the project chronology, on-chain record structure and distinct LFGK/NBG tokens. See the seven-collection registry for exact source-linked account names.

Graffiti Kings' former commercial website was graffitikings.co.uk. Browse its December 2019 homepage and November 2019 work index for the physical-wall, workshop, film and advertising work that predated the blockchain chapter.
