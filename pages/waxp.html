WAXP & GRAFFITI KINGS

The blockchain infrastructure, account resources, WAXP currency, NFTs, collector history and project boundaries behind Graffiti Kings, GKniftyHEADS, Crypto Moonboys and GraffPUNKS.

WAXP: the network asset beneath GK's digital art

WAXP is the native asset of the WAX blockchain. It was not created or issued by Graffiti Kings, Darren Cullen, GKniftyHEADS, GraffPUNKS or Crypto Moonboys. Those projects use WAX as one blockchain on which NFT collection identities, transactions and creator-led economies can be published. WAX official token guide describes WAXP's role in transfers, NFT and application activity, staking and network governance.

WAX names the network; WAXP is the widely used exchange and market ticker for its native token. On WAX mainnet, asset data and trading pairs may identify the native coin as WAX issued by eosio.token. Alcor's market data documentation explicitly illustrates wax-eosio.token; this is a chain/contract/symbol reference, not a separate Graffiti Kings coin. Always check the receiving chain and contract rather than relying on an exchange label alone.

Core connection: Graffiti Kings = the street-art movement; GKniftyHEADS = the named character/identity NFT programme; Crypto Moonboys = the wider creator umbrella; GraffPUNKS = a linked art, publishing and radio culture; WAX = the blockchain and WAXP = its native network asset. An NFT is a record on that infrastructure, not WAXP itself.

What WAXP actually does: trade, resources, stake, vote

Commerce: WAXP can be used in network-native marketplaces and related trades. Some items are quoted in WAX, while others use project-issued tokens or alternative supported currencies; always confirm the specific listing. Account operations: the native asset is involved in funding accounts and obtaining the resources needed to sign and execute on-chain actions. Governance: WAX holders can take part in the chain's proof-of-stake voting and staking processes. These roles come from the chain itself, not a Graffiti Kings entitlement.

WAX's account-funding guide lists exchange/on-ramp acquisition, transfers from another WAX account and application distributions as common acquisition routes. It warns that transfers must use the correct network, recipient name and memo rules. Funding an account alone is not proof that an NFT redemption or game is live.

CPU, NET, RAM and PowerUp — why an NFT transfer may fail

WAX uses a resource allocation model, rather than a straightforward Ethereum-style gas charge for each action. CPU is the execution capacity consumed by transactions and contracts; NET represents transaction bandwidth; RAM provides on-chain storage for account and contract state. WAX's core resources guide explains why account activity and software design must budget for all three.

PowerUp is a method of renting temporary CPU and NET. It is useful when a collector has sufficient assets but lacks the execution resources needed to transfer, list, burn or blend them. RAM is a separate purchased storage resource; PowerUp is not a substitute for project contract storage planning. Read WAX's practical PowerUp guide.

For Graffiti Kings, this is the difference between owning an NFT in a wallet and successfully performing an action on that NFT. An auction listing, blend, burn, transfer or third-party game interaction may need permissions and resources even when the image can be viewed freely. Resource shortages, contract rejections and endpoint outages are different causes; never assume the wallet is faulty without checking the transaction details.

WAX accounts, My Cloud Wallet, Anchor and security

The WAX account is the on-chain identity. A wallet is software or a sign-in method that manages authorisation for that identity. The official WAX wallets guide describes My Cloud Wallet as a consumer-friendly route and Anchor as a tool for advanced account/key and permission management. They are different interfaces to WAX, not separate NFT blockchains.

For a new collector: create or recover a WAX mainnet account through an official route, secure recovery information offline, confirm the precise account name, understand CPU/NET/RAM, fund modestly where needed and review each requested wallet action. For a creator/operator: secure owner authority, use restricted active/custom permissions for appropriate workflows, and never expose signing keys in Discord, Telegram, web forms or public repositories. The official security and recovery guide stresses transaction-review and permission separation.

Wallet warning: WAX documentation names official Anchor guidance and explicitly warns against lookalike domains. Do not choose a wallet from an unsolicited advert or link. Wallet support and on-ramp availability can change over time; check the official source before installing or transferring.

How an artwork becomes a WAX NFT

WAX's AtomicAssets design organises NFTs in four layers: collection → schema → template → asset. A collection identifies a project or curator. The schema describes the fields used by a category of artwork. The template supplies repeatable metadata for editions, while an individual asset has its own on-chain identity and ownership. WAX's AtomicAssets guide also explains mutable versus immutable data and collection-level authorisation.

For a Graffiti Kings NFT, an investigator can ask: Which collection issued it? What schema and template does it use? How many assets were minted under that template? Who owns the individual asset? What transfer, listing or burn activity is visible? Is the published artwork media stored on a durable public storage system such as IPFS, and can it still be retrieved? Each is a different piece of provenance.

An image is not a licence. Blockchain provenance can identify a token and its transaction record, but does not by itself prove the artist's offline identity, legal copyright assignment, project solvency or a working game entitlement. For any GKniftyHEADS 1/1 creator-rights claim, consult the exact asset-specific published licence rather than assuming every edition carries identical permissions. See the creator ownership boundary.

Graffiti Kings' move from physical walls to WAX

Graffiti before the blockchain. Darren Cullen / SER's Graffiti Kings network developed through public murals, youth work, commercial commissions and street-art culture. Those activities predate WAX; the chain did not create the movement. Read the documented SER/Graffiti Kings history.

Early NFT period. GraffPUNKS retrospectively dates its WAX giveaway initiative to May 2020, and makes large circulation/recipient claims. Those are historical first-party figures, not independently counted by this guide. 2021. The Graffiti Queens exhibition in Decentraland and other reported metaverse activity developed a separate NFT-exhibition strand. Decentraland and the WAX mainnet should not be mistaken for one chain or one asset programme; see the 2021 exhibition record.

2022. Blockleaders' August 2022 report independently connected Cullen and Graffiti Kings to WAX NFTs, LFGK and Telegram/game projects as described at that date. Later Alien Worlds grant-recipient reporting identifies Cullen and Ian Harrison on a planned HODL Warriors game collaboration; that announcement does not establish final NFT issuance. Read the SYNC/Alien Worlds source record.

Later development. The project grew into named WAX collections, the broader Crypto Moonboys creator universe, game interfaces, Telegram participation and specialist community tokens. These do not imply that all older whitepaper features or game rewards are presently operational. Use the maintained ecosystem source map and the current official project interface for live features.

What each GK project does on the WAX layer

  • Graffiti Kings: physical street-art lineage and the archive of artist/collector editions. WAX provides collectible identity and transfer records, not the history itself.
  • GKniftyHEADS: character-based WAX NFTs, editions, selected 1/1 identities and related faction artefacts. Verify rights and templates individually.
  • Crypto Moonboys: a broader creator platform using NFT and game-linked artefacts where currently supported; some activity is off-chain and Telegram-scored.
  • GraffPUNKS: art, music, radio and publishing context, with proposals for NFTs and specialised community tokens. The 3008 Paint Path is fiction.
  • NO BALL GAMES: a separate Charlie Buster project with noballgamess WAX NFTs and a proposed WAX-based $NBG token, alongside different XRPL-based XRP KIDs records. See its distinct asset/fiction dossier.
  • Bitcoin Kid and collaborator drops: named collections and external product proposals, including the Invasion Toys vinyl-to-NFT announcement. Proposed minting targets and physical redemptions are not treated as fulfilled orders.

WAXP versus LFGK, NBG, PUNK, WUF and other tokens

The distinction between a native network coin and a contract-issued project token matters. WAXP/WAX underpins blockchain resources and transactions. LFGK is a Graffiti Kings-associated community token; $NBG belongs to NO BALL GAMES; $PUNK and other names have appeared in GraffPUNKS promotional or planning material; WUF is a separate WUFFI community token. None should be labelled WAXP or assumed exchangeable one-for-one with it.

WAX's tokens documentation identifies native WAXP separately from fungible assets issued under smart contracts. On marketplaces or decentralised exchanges, always check contract, ticker, precision, network and current liquidity. Two tokens can share a short name. Never assume a projected tokenomics document identifies an active market or a current payout programme.

A historic project page may describe a staking reward, an NFT burn, a radio reward or an in-game currency. That does not independently verify present contract state, exchange depth, withdrawal restrictions or final NFT supply. For project statements, consult Crypto Moonboys' WAXP page and the GraffPUNKS project description; for chain-level mechanics use WAX documentation.

Games, Telegram engagement and community rewards

Graffiti Kings and Crypto Moonboys have public-facing games, character tools and community spaces. A project may use on-chain NFTs as collectibles, event rewards, burn ingredients, identity markers or game-recognised assets. But the underlying WAX network does not automatically send rewards for owning an NFT, playing a website game or posting in Telegram.

Any automatic draw, WAX account registration, token inventory, staking programme or cash-out depends on a separately deployed project service, its current configuration, funded balance, eligibility rules and an authorised signer. Rankings and site XP may also be separate from blockchain balances. Avoid equating an off-chain score with an immediately transferable token balance. The official Crypto Moonboys Arcade hub and umbrella project wiki are the starting points for presently published features.

When a project says “5%” or “daily rewards,” always ask 5% of what, held where, at what snapshot, distributed by which contract and subject to which cap? An illustrative entitlement is not a funded transaction. Do not publish a payout figure as completed without a transaction ID and confirmed chain record.

WAXP staking and governance are chain-level participation

WAX uses delegated proof-of-stake governance. WAXP holders may delegate or stake tokens and vote for block producers under the network's current system. The rewards and unstaking rules belong to WAX itself and can evolve; they are not Graffiti Kings royalties. Official WAXP use cases and the WAX getting-started section explain the chain participation routes.

Staking a token in a third-party product, depositing assets to a liquidity pool, burning an NFT for a game item, and voting with native WAXP are different operations with different custody and liquidity risks. Do not use the word “staking” to imply all those mechanisms are interchangeable or guaranteed to return a profit.

A practical verification checklist for collectors and researchers

  1. Find the exact issuing collection account and check whether the NFT is a genuine asset from that collection rather than a similarly named copy.
  2. Open its collection, schema, template and individual asset records. Compare the displayed artist/project information with a direct source, not only a marketplace badge.
  3. Inspect mint counts, transfers, burns, authorised minters and other contract activity at a suitable explorer/indexer. Remember that indexers can be temporarily behind the chain.
  4. When buying or transferring, confirm the correct WAX mainnet account name, token contract, currency, fee/resource prompt and signing permission. A WAXP exchange balance is not automatically a WAX NFT wallet.
  5. For a 1/1 licence, access pass, game item, NFT blend or physical redemption, read the current asset-specific terms and check whether the promised service still exists.
  6. For quoted prices, pool liquidity, payouts, user totals or wallet balances, use time-stamped public records and avoid implying that older prices, current holdings or projected valuations are guaranteed.

Independent browsing starting points: WAX official marketplace and explorer directory, NFT Hive's GKniftyHEADS record and the seven-account registry. The exact assets and third-party market status are not certified by this wiki.

Security, permissions, ownership and market risk

Market: WAXP is volatile. NFT asking prices and speculative token markets do not establish realisable value. Liquidity may be limited, and a listing is not a completed sale. Custody: transfers and burns can be irreversible; wrong-network withdrawals may be unrecoverable. Permissions: a wallet signature can authorise more than a simple website login. Review transaction actions, spender, permission level and destination before approval. Infrastructure: explorers, market indexers, wallet providers and game backends can fail even when the blockchain is available.

Creative rights: owning an NFT is not automatically owning the copyright to its artwork, an artist's brand or every future commercial use. The current Crypto Moonboys creator framework distinguishes qualifying unique 1/1 licences from generic NFTs and separates creator ownership from community canon acceptance. Historical claims: GraffPUNKS' published numbers for giveaways, token rewards and burns may be aspirational or unreconciled. This page does not endorse purchasing a token, minting an asset or trusting an unverified future reward.

Security reference: WAX official security and recovery documentation. When visiting an external project, start from verified official sources and never provide a recovery phrase, private key or full owner-authority signature to a web form.
