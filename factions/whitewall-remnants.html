WHITEWALL REMNANTS

The leftover traces of erased walls, scrubbed tags and culture-cleansing systems

Canon Role

The Whitewall Remnants are part of the Watchers, Prophets and Mystery Factions. They represent what remains after culture is painted over, deleted, sanitised or rendered blank. In a universe built from graffiti, memory and chain records, the act of whitewashing is never neutral. It creates ghosts.

The Remnants are not simply enemies. They are evidence. Every cleaned wall, erased piece and overwritten memory leaves a residue. This faction is that residue made visible.

Function in the Story

The Whitewall Remnants force the Grid to confront erasure. Their existence reminds the creative factions that the enemy is not always loud. Sometimes the threat is blankness, tidiness, smoothing, institutional repainting and the slow removal of street memory.

They haunt the edge between oppression and preservation. Some Remnants may carry the marks of lost tags. Others may act like sterile wall-skin trying to cover living culture again. The faction should remain unsettling rather than simple.

Connection to The Whitewasher

The Whitewall Remnants are thematically connected to The Whitewasher character, but the faction and the character should not be collapsed into the same thing unless later canon confirms it. The character may embody whitewashing as force. The faction represents what is left behind after that force passes through.

Visual Language

Use pale paint, scraped texture, ghost outlines, hidden letterforms, roller marks, washed-out stencils and wall scars. The look should feel like a graffiti wall after the council has tried and failed to erase its memory.

Canon Boundaries

Do not turn the Whitewall Remnants into a generic villain army. Their value is symbolic: erased culture becoming trace, trace becoming warning, warning becoming faction.
